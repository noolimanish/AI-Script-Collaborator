[VIBE: poetic, devastating, quiet tension, violent redemption, deeply emotional]
[CORE RELATIONSHIP: Aryan (a gangster trying to change) and Aaravi (a grieving daughter seeking revenge).]
[THEME: Love versus blood. The choice to abandon violence for the sake of a memory.]
[KEY MOTIFS: Tomato stew, red carnations, fire, the café, the grave under glass.]

THE BOOK OF AARAVI
One bullet for justice. One for love.

1  ·  The Bouquet at Dusk  (present‑day, 1949)
Black‑and‑white drizzle, a cobbled street, the camera fixed behind a lone man in a dark suit.In his fist: a bouquet of wilting red carnations that provide the only colour in the frame.
He faces a warm little café whose sign glows crimson:
AARAVI’S TABLE — TOMATO STEWS & SOUL FOOD
Inside, every accent is a shade of tomato: dusky reds on the walls, scarlet‑ink sketches, sun‑dried slices pressed under glass.He steps in, breath catching the aroma of coriander–laced stew.
At the quietest corner, beneath a skylight, a marble grave rests under protective glass.He kneels, lays the flowers, whispers:
ARYAN (low)Happy twentieth, Aaravi … The stew’s still yours. The smile too.
From the kitchen a girl of nineteen appears, flour on her cheek, carrying a steaming bowl.
GIRL“Papa, taste this. I let the tomatoes kiss the pan before the broth—just like she did.”
He smiles through a shiver of grief.This is also Aaravi—his adopted daughter and the café’s head chef, raised on the legend of a mother she never met.

✦ Scene 2: The Smell of Gunpowder — 20 Years Earlier
Boom.Muffled shots ring through the alley. Crates topple. A rival gang lies motionless.
Aryan, in a black shirt soaked in sweat and rage, reloads calmly.A final man begs.
GANGSTER (pleading)“I didn’t touch your shipment—!”
Aryan shoots.No hesitation. No mercy.
He wipes his face. Hands trembling slightly.
ARYAN (to his bodyguard)“Coffee. That old spot. I need a minute.”

✦ Scene 3: Across the Street — First Glimpse of Her
Aryan sits down at a tiny outdoor café, just around the corner from the slaughter.
As he takes a sip, his eyes drift across the street.
There she is — a young woman, hair tied up messily, lugging a sack of tomatoes into a noisy warehouse that sells raw food and daily supplies.
She’s not part of the café.She’s a server — wiping sweat, clearing crates, occasionally slapped by her supervisor for no reason.
ARYAN (to himself)“She looks... like she doesn’t belong here.”
Something about her catches him.Her silence? Her struggle?He doesn’t know. But he watches.

✦ Scene 4: Nightfall — The Second Encounter
That night, Aryan returns for coffee again.Same café. Same alley. This time he lingers longer.
The street glows in warm lights now.
Across from the warehouse is a small food truck — beaten up, yet glowing bright from the inside.
She’s there.
Cooking.Serving.Sweating.Smiling.
A line of people wait eagerly. She hands them bowls of tomato stew like they’re bowls of home.
ARYAN (intrigued)“That’s… her?”

✦ Scene 5: The Stew That Broke Him
Back in the car, Aryan speaks without looking:
ARYAN“Get me a bowl. From her. That food truck.”
The bodyguard hesitates.
BODYGUARD“From that stall, boss? You serious?”
Minutes later, Aryan tastes it.
And freezes.
ARYAN“It tastes like a… hug. Or something close.”

✦ Scene 6: A Silent Guardian Begins
Over the next few days…
He watches her from shadows.He follows her.He sees:
– Her landlord screaming at her for being late on rent.– Her cart breaking down.– Drunk men troubling her at night.
And each time, she’s helped. But she never sees by whom.
– The rent gets paid.– A new tire arrives mysteriously.– A man gets beaten badly near the cart, and no one knows why.
She prays.
AARAVI (hands folded)“Thank you, God… Whoever you sent... thank you.”
Aryan watches… and turns away slowly.
He is falling. Hard.
ARYAN (voiceover)“I’ve killed men without flinching.But watching you suffer…makes my hands tremble.”

✦ Scene 7: A Crash of Tomatoes
Daytime again. The alley bustles with vendors shouting prices, fruit sellers splashing water on their produce. Aaravi pushes a heavy crate of ripe tomatoes toward her food truck.
It slips.
The crate crashes.
Tomatoes scatter like spilled rubies across the street. Her apron gets soaked in red pulp.
A few vendors laugh. One scoffs:
VENDOR (mocking)“Maybe if you smiled less and worked more, you’d keep them in the box!”
She kneels, trying to collect what’s salvageable.
Just then — a pair of polished black shoes stop beside her.
She looks up… sees him for the first time, up close.Aryan kneels too, silently helping her gather the tomatoes.
She stares at him suspiciously.
AARAVI (wary)“You don’t look like a tomato man.”
ARYAN (smirking)“I’m learning. They seem… fragile. Like some people.”
She holds one up and squints.
AARAVI“You bruise them once, they rot twice as fast.”
He looks at her a moment too long. The street noise fades behind them.
ARYAN“Good lesson.”
She stands, takes the crate.
AARAVI“Thanks, stranger.”
ARYAN“Aryan.”
She hesitates.
AARAVI“Not asking.”
She walks away.
Aryan smiles slightly, watching her vanish into the food truck cloud.

✦ Scene 8: First Name, First Stirring
That evening, she’s serving stew again. The line is long.
Suddenly, a bowl is pushed toward her from the front — with a note instead of money.
She opens it.It simply says:
“Let the tomatoes kiss the flame. Stir counter-clockwise. Trust me. — Aryan”
She looks up. He’s standing at the end of the line, arms folded. Calm.Not smiling.
She frowns, curious.
AARAVI (softly to herself)“Stranger has a tongue too, huh?”

✦ Scene 9: The Lie that Feels Like Safety
Later that week, they meet again — this time under the awning, late at night, during a drizzle. No one else around.
She leans against the cart. He stands across.
AARAVI“You… eat a lot for someone who doesn’t look hungry.”
ARYAN“You serve a lot for someone always out of ingredients.”
They both half-smile.
Silence sits with them like an old friend.
She finally asks:
AARAVI“So what do you do, really?”
He hesitates.
A flash of gunfire in his memory.
ARYAN (lying)“Business. Imports. Not tomatoes, though.”
AARAVI (shrugs)“As long as it’s not bullets and blood.”
Aryan stiffens for half a second.
ARYAN (quietly)“I’m trying to leave all that behind.”
She doesn’t press. Instead, she turns to her pot.
AARAVI“You ever stir stew before?”
ARYAN“Only when it’s worth burning for.”
She lets him step close. She hands him the ladle.
The silence isn’t awkward anymore.
It’s warm

✦ Scene 13: No Name, Just This
(Months pass — a quiet, wordless relationship blossoms)
The city doesn’t notice, but Aryan is changing.
No more nightly violence. Fewer calls from his father.More late evenings near a tomato-scented food truck, more mornings watching her laugh with strangers and children.

Montage Sequence — “Subtle Love”
• She burns a batch of stew — he gently stirs the next one with her.
• He brings her a new lantern for the cart — she teases:AARAVI: "Do you bring gifts to all women who drop tomatoes?"
• She stitches his torn shirt one day. He stares at her hands in silence.
• They walk home on rainy nights, sharing a broken umbrella.
• They watch people eat together — not saying a word, but knowing they’re together too.
There is no confession. No kiss.Just presence. And peace.

✦ Scene 14: Aryan’s Slow Goodbye to the Darkness
At his safehouse, Aryan sits in front of his gun cabinet.One by one, he locks weapons away. His bodyguard watches silently.
ARYAN (softly)"Every time she stirs that stew......I want to forget what my hands have done."
He transfers money out of illegal accounts.Stops showing up to meetings.
His father Raghav Malik gets a call.
GOON (on phone)"Sir, he’s not visiting the eastern docks. Missed last three dropoffs. Rumor is… he's hanging around some girl."
Raghav hangs up. Cold. Controlled.
RAGHAV (to his right hand)"My son doesn’t fall in love.He falls in line."

✦ Scene 15: Shadows on the Wall
One night, Aaravi steps out of her cart to find a man across the street smoking.
She doesn’t recognize him — but he’s watching too closely.
Another night, her wheel is slashed.Before she can panic, a replacement arrives within the hour — like usual.
But this time, Aryan finds a folded note tucked under the wheel.
“She’s becoming a weakness. You’re forgetting who you are.”
Aryan clenches it.

✦ Scene 16: His Father’s Warning
Back at Raghav’s mansion. Rain pours on the roof like bullets.
Aryan arrives, face quiet.
Raghav sits at the head of a long table, swirling a glass of scotch.
RAGHAV"You miss family dinners, miss collections, miss meetings. But you show up the minute I whistle."
Aryan doesn’t respond.
RAGHAV (leaning in)"So who is she? Some girl in a cart who smiles nice?"
ARYAN"She’s none of your business."
RAGHAV (coldly)"Everything you love is my business."
He lights a cigar.
RAGHAV"I built an empire of fear. You think stew and soft eyes can feed that hunger?"
Aryan turns to leave.
RAGHAV (calling out)"Remember, boy — love doesn’t make you pure. It just makes you vulnerable."

✦ Scene 17: She Starts Feeling It Too
Aaravi starts to notice:
• A black car parked nearby too often.
• A man she saw once in Aryan’s shadow now watching her alone.
• Aryan becoming quieter… a little distant. Not because he doesn’t care — but because he’s protecting her from something she doesn’t know yet.

Dialogue (Night outside her truck):
AARAVI (tentative)"You disappear sometimes… not just in place, but in your face."
ARYAN (quietly)"Because I’m scared. That if I stay too close... the past will catch up."
AARAVI"Then let it come. I don’t even know what you were.But I know what you are when you're with me."
She rests her head on his shoulder for the first time.

✦ Scene 18: The Van Without a Name
Aaravi closes her food truck. It’s late. She yawns, dragging a crate of leftover tomatoes into the warehouse behind her.
Suddenly — a van screeches.Two men in black pull her in. A cloth is pressed to her mouth.
AARAVI (muffled)“N-No! Let me go!”
She kicks. A tomato crate crashes to the ground. The van disappears.

✦ Scene 19: The Call from the Trunk
The world outside blurs into streetlight streaks and muffled horns.
Inside the trunk of a moving van, Aaravi lies curled between crates and burlap sacks, her breath shallow, her knees scraped. She’s been crying — but her eyes burn more with anger than fear now.
The van stinks of rust and rotting onions. It's dark. Claustrophobic.
Her fingers tremble, but she slips her hand into her pocket.
Her phone. It’s still there.Screen cracked. Signal weak.
She dials the only name in her world that feels like safety:
Aryan.
A moment’s pause… then his voice.
ARYAN (sharp, alert)“Aaravi?”
AARAVI (whispers, barely holding it together)“Aryan... I—I’m in the trunk of a van. The same man from the other night… the one who kept watching me... they took me.”
ARYAN (immediate, low)“Where are you now?”
AARAVI (straining to hear through engine noise)“I don’t know. The road’s bumpy… I smell fish… maybe near the docks. I think we passed something that sounded like a train.”
Her voice cracks.
AARAVI (quietly)“I’m scared.”
ARYAN (steady, but furious underneath)“Stay with me. I’ve already called the police. They’re coming for you.”
He ends the call calmly.
Then turns to his men — rage blooming in his eyes like wildfire.
ARYAN (to his gang)“Find that van. Tail it. Crash it if you have to — but don’t let them see you coming. Get her out.”
MAN“Police?”
ARYAN (dead serious)“No police. No mercy. Make it look like a gang hit.”
He walks off, breath heavy, jaw tight.
ARYAN (under his breath)“If they touch her… I’ll burn their bloodlines.”

✦ Scene 20: Blood in the Shadows
Raghav’s men laugh, mocking her.
Suddenly — lights cut.Gunshots.Screams.One of the gang members falls through a window.
Masked men storm in — fast, brutal.
In under three minutes, it’s chaos.
One of them unties her roughly, shoves her toward the back alley.
MASKED MAN (gruffly)“Run. Police are coming.”
She stumbles out, breath shaky.

✦ Scene 21: The Hug
She reaches the main road. A figure waits in the shadows.
Aryan.
She sees him and without a word — she runs into his arms.
AARAVI (breathless)“They said they’d… they’d kill me. I thought it was the end.”
She buries her face in his chest. Trembling.
Aryan doesn’t move. His arms are frozen.
Because in that moment — the man who has never known peace... is being held by it.
And he's terrified to hold it back — afraid he'll break her.
ARYAN (whispers)“I’m here. You’re safe.”
She steps back. Tears drying.
AARAVI (quietly)“I’m sorry. I don’t usually… fall apart like that.”
He smiles faintly.
ARYAN“You didn’t fall. You just landed.”
She looks away, grateful — unaware that her heart… and his… are now bound tighter than they both understand.

✦ Scene 22: The Secret That Grows in Her Heart
Days later.
She’s quiet. Calmer — but her eyes linger on shadows longer.
She researches things on paper — no internet in this era — scraps of anatomy diagrams, newspaper clippings, crime sketches.
At night, she practices with a kitchen knife behind her food truck. Just simple moves. Basic stabs.
She tells herself it’s for self-defense.
But in her heart — the face she imagines when she thrusts the knife…

AARAVI (to herself)
“One clean strike. Just once. That’s all I need.”


24 – "The Fire That Ties Us"
EXT. OLD WAREHOUSE STREET – AFTERNOON

The wind drags dust across the rusting roads. Inside a half-broken warehouse, AARAVI is lifting crates alone. Sweat beads her brow. A calm, routine day.
Suddenly, she hears something—a soft rumble growing louder.
A black luxury car zooms past the warehouse.

She freezes.

Eyes sharpen.

Not a word. Not a thought.

She grabs her old bicycle, wheels creaking, and rides after it. Fast.

We don’t know why she’s chasing it.
We don’t know who she saw.
But her eyes… they carry a storm.

EXT. VILLA DRIVEWAY – LATE EVENING
She watches from a distance.

The car enters a high-walled estate. She drops the cycle far behind, crouches near the gate. Her breath shallow.

A man steps out of the car.

Tall. Sharp suit. Salt-gray hair.

RAGHAV MALIK.

She doesn’t recognize him yet—not fully.

She sees only a face from her nightmares. A ghost she’s waited years to meet.


INT. VILLA – HALLWAY – NIGHT
She enters quietly through a broken side gate. Creeps in like a shadow.

Her fingers tighten around a small kitchen knife hidden in her coat.

She glances up.


She walks down a corridor—eyes locked onto Raghav, standing by a drinks cabinet, alone.

One breath. One step.

She raises the knife—

But a guard’s voice pierces the silence.

“INTRUDER!”

Two men pounce on her. The knife clatters away. Fists fly. Boots slam into her ribs. She screams as blood smears the marble.

 The Promise & The Lie
Months later, Aryan finds a crying infant outside a burnt‑out mission.He names her Aaravi—to keep the first promise he ever broke.
To let the child stay near, he spins a gentle fiction:
“Your mother was the bravest woman I knew.She left her recipes for you in my heart.”
He sells every gun, buys the alley where the food cart once stood, builds a café bathed in tomato hues and memory.He hires ex‑thugs, widows, war orphans—any shattered soul willing to trade violence for warmth.
And every year, on the night the fire took his love, he lays flowers over the grave set into the café floor; a grave no city can erase.



 Stew for the Soul  (back to present)
The daughter sets the bowl between them.Steam spirals up like a ghost resolved to linger kindly.
DAUGHTER AARAVI“Was she really so fearless, Papa?”
Aryan’s eyes glisten; truth and lie wrestle in his throat.
ARYAN“Fearless… and forgiving.She asked me to live for love, not blood.Everything good in me—everything gentle in you—came from her.”
She tastes the stew, eyes shining.

Aryan looks toward the grave behind glass.One red carnation slips from the bouquet, landing on the marble like a final blush.
He whispers—not to the past now, but forward through time:
ARYAN“Forgive me, beloved.I have chosen life, as you asked.My weapon is a spoon. My kingdom, a kitchen.Our story… served warm, every night.”
Fade to the hush of clinking cutlery, the soft hum of a café that smells of tomatoes, coriander, and redemption—the only flavours strong enough to outlive fire and regret.
